# Study in Denmark

The independently runnable stdlib adapter collects seven genuine named funding
frameworks substantively described by the official Danish ministry publisher.
It excludes directory-only data, university/course clones, images and applications.

```sh
python -B test_adapter.py
```

The sole live collection test emits complete actual JSON on stdout and diagnostics
on stderr. It never publishes or requires a publication token. Only the matching
main-branch Action publishes this source's data/metadata atomically.

## Inventory and identity

Six own scholarship-page frameworks: Danish government scholarships, Nordplus,
Erasmus, Erasmus Mundus/Joint Master Degree, Fulbright Denmark and Danish State
Educational Support (SU). The own PhD/research page additionally gives a named
Industrial PhD grant, including applicant, employment, purpose and procedure.
These are seven actual programme identities, with original publisher URLs;
shared article URLs do not erase distinct named frameworks.

The complete fresh source research inspected 14 own HTML pages, robots and the
advertised sitemap index/918-route child. The full news SSR inventory contains
22 actual news identities plus two images; the visible ten cards alone would
be incomplete. All 22 descriptions concern admissions, guidance, residence
rules, culture or rankings. Current events inventory has zero results. Master's,
exchange and summer-school leaves add no new funded award: Erasmus is deduplicated,
unnamed bilateral exchanges and degree/course guidance are excluded.

There is no external provider/university catalogue enumeration. University grant
allocations, available courses, countries, generic PhD degree structures,
Aarhus scholarship examples, salary examples, old open days and job-bank links
never become synthetic current awards. The statutory free-tuition rules are
ordinary legal fee treatment, separate from government scholarship eligibility.

## Countries and eligibility

Known Danish hosts are government scholarships, Erasmus, Erasmus Mundus,
Fulbright, SU and Industrial PhD. Erasmus's own exchange leaf explicitly offers
incoming study in Denmark, requiring higher education enrolment in the student's
country of residence. Broader abroad scope is preserved: DK is a known host, not
an assertion that Denmark is the whole scheme's only destination. Mundus's
other European partner country is unspecified. Nordplus states Nordic/Baltic
mobility without a complete named country list; its host list remains unknown.

All positive citizenship lists remain empty. Government scholarship explicitly
requires citizenship outside EU/EEA/Switzerland, full-degree enrolment and a
time-limited education residence permit. Its introductory Swiss wording conflicts
with the specific exclusion: the explicit eligibility bullet controls and the
conflict is retained. Exclusions also include artistic admission, legal claim
to Danish-citizen rights, SU eligibility and the narrowly specified §9c(1)
child/parent §9m residence condition. This is not exclusion of every resident.
The tuition page's different §9m/§9a rules are not merged into these conditions.

Nordplus affiliation and Erasmus's students-from wording are not citizenship
whitelists. Fulbright's own exact descriptor is American scholar or master's/PhD
postgraduate; the paragraph does not define a passport/residence test. SU residence
and possible equal status under Danish/EU rules do not prove every foreign student
qualifies or create DK-only nationality eligibility.

Industrial PhD's private company applies to Innovation Fund Denmark on the
candidate's behalf; the employed researcher is beneficiary, and university
PhD enrolment follows grant success. Pay follows the company agreement. The
page's DKK32,567 Copenhagen average faculty-funded salary, DKK50,000 general
tuition fee and generic 3+5/4+4 examples are not this award's benefits or promised
eligibility. No precise grant amount is invented.

All seven are genuine framework overviews with no dated own call: deadline null,
status unknown. Annual allocations, recurring admission deadlines and execution
year never become application closing dates. No current open award is claimed.

Each of 14 required pages must be complete and match reviewed material conditions
or actual own navigation/news inventory. Full news item totals must agree with
the complete embedded actual inventory; a new current event requires review.
The advertised sitemap index and relevant own funding/policy route inventory
must match; unrelated course/image changes are excluded. New rights statements,
linked terms/licences or changed ministry attribution fail closed. Unrelated
call-to-action programme counts and protected image metadata are omitted from
factual fingerprints, so volatile UI is not mistaken for an award condition.

[Cookies](https://studyindenmark.dk/privacy) is cookie information, not a content
licence. The government footer is provenance, not blanket copyright permission.
No express factual extraction/reuse restriction was found in inspected actual
source pages/navigation/sitemap. Publish attributed original factual summaries
and links; no images, protected full prose or claimed open licence. No publisher
contact or application submission was performed. Malformed internal backend links
are not requested or published; every record points to the actual public source.

All source requests share a locked publisher-family allowance: at least six
seconds between starts, at most ten in a rolling 60 seconds, including redirects,
retries and overlapping local processes. Robots and Retry-After are honored;
normal identifying user agent, TLS and session proxy are preserved. Publication
runs for this source are serialized; do not overlap a local source test with remote runs.

First failure creates no source folder. After success, fetch/parse/validation
failure preserves last-good data and the success timestamp, recording sanitized
failure metadata when possible. Atomic, non-forced source-scoped commits preserve
sibling updates and refuse to overwrite newer concurrent data of this same source.

## Bounded child-sitemap recovery

During independent QA, one complete source test failed child-sitemap validation
with invalid/external locations. The failing XML was not retained, so its exact
cause is unknown. One authorized diagnostic at the identical endpoint then
returned valid XML with all 918 locations on the reviewed HTTPS source origin.
This does not prove which values were invalid or establish an IP/proxy cause.

The runtime permits one same-endpoint re-retrieval only after invalid/malformed
child XML metadata, delayed at least 30 seconds. Ordinary publisher spacing stays
six seconds; a longer shared Retry-After deadline still wins. Both attempts must
pass complete strict XML/origin and reviewed funding/policy inventory validation.
A repeated invalid result fails closed. Valid changed funding/policy inventory,
HTTP refusal and HTML/XML challenge documents fail immediately without this
retry. Foreign/internal locations are never accepted or fetched. Diagnostics
contain counts and up to three scheme/host classes only, without userinfo, paths,
queries, full XML or claimed original failure cause.

## Expired-record cleanup

A successful refresh validates the complete nonempty collected inventory, then excludes records whose supported `deadline` has passed before preparing the atomic source data/metadata replacement. Previously published records are used only to preserve matching records' first-seen timestamps; they are never merged back into the replacement. Thus expired old records cannot survive a successful replacement. Missing deadlines, ambiguous clocks, status, age and absence from the response are not expiry evidence.

Explicit timezone-aware deadlines expire strictly after their instant. For date-only deadlines without a documented publisher timezone, cleanup waits until the closing day has ended in every legal timezone: the current date in UTC-12 must be later than the closing date. This conservative boundary retains the full closing day without inventing a publisher timezone; deletion can be delayed up to 26 hours relative to the earliest civil closing-day boundary. Unsupported or ambiguous source clocks remain null deadlines; invalid timestamp fields fail validation before cleanup. If every collected record is expired, the valid nonempty retrieval publishes an empty successful replacement. A later empty upstream retrieval still fails and preserves that last-good pair.

Fetch, parse, validation and publication failures preserve last-good data and success timestamps. Cleanup performs no separate deletion or remote write; publication updates only this source's data/metadata atomically, with the existing concurrent-update protection. The live test collects real inventory without publication; offline boundary checks and static publication review verify cleanup.

## Daily automation

The scheduled refresh runs once daily at **08:47 UTC** (`47 8 * * *`).
This fixed time was selected randomly within a staggered daily timetable; it
is not randomized again on each run. Main-merge and manual triggers remain
available, so this is one scheduled run per day, not a hard limit on all runs.
GitHub can delay scheduled events; the existing publication concurrency
serializes source jobs when they queue. Existing disabled workflows remain
disabled until their access hold is separately resolved.

## Workflow concurrency

`youthopps-dk-study-in-denmark-publisher-v1` serializes runs of this source. Up
to 100 pending runs are retained (`queue: max`); active runs are not cancelled

publish in parallel. Existing source-only atomic publication rebases on
unrelated repository updates, uses up to three publication attempts, and never
force-overwrites newer data.
